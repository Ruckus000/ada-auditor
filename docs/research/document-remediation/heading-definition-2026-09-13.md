# What a heading is — the labelling definition, from the standards

**Date:** 2026-09-13. **Status:** FROZEN 2026-09-13 (user approval). Rulings change only by
adding a row to §5 with the case that forced it; nothing is edited in place. Every quotation below was fetched and checked on this date;
nothing is paraphrased from memory. A source that could not be fetched is
named as such and not relied on.

This is the definition a reviewer labels against and the checker later grades
against. It exists because "is this a heading?" is settled at the edges by the
PDF standards' *vocabulary*, not by WCAG's *obligation*, and every model error
in the Qwen spikes (Parts 1–28) fell on one of those edges.

## 1. The obligation — WCAG 2.2

**1.3.1 Info and Relationships (Level A).** *"Information, structure, and
relationships conveyed through presentation can be programmatically determined
or are available in text."* Its Understanding page names the cue we are
reading: *"headings are often in a larger, bold font separated from paragraphs
by blank lines."* Two of its failure techniques are our two error classes:

- **F2** — *"a change in the appearance of text conveys meaning without using
  appropriate semantic markup."* Example: `<p class="heading1">Introduction</p>`
  fails because *"assistive technology could not distinguish it as a heading."*
  This is the **false negative**: a heading left as `P`.
- **F43** — *"structural markup is used to achieve a presentational effect, but
  indicates relationships that do not exist in the content."* Example 1 is a
  heading element used to display *an address* in large bold type without
  starting a section; Example 2 is `h3`/`h4` used *"only for visual effect — to
  control the fonts used to display the authors' names and the date."* This is
  the **false positive**: a non-heading tagged `H*`. Its test is the one
  sentence a reviewer applies: *"the element's semantic meaning is exposed to
  assistive technology and appropriate for the content of the element."*

**2.4.6 Headings and Labels (Level AA).** *"Headings and labels describe topic
or purpose."* It *"does not require headings or labels"* to exist. It governs
the **quality** of a heading, not whether something is one — a separate
question, already a separate punch item (the 2.4.6 prose-headings measurement).
Not part of this label.

**2.4.10 Section Headings (Level AAA).** Outside the AA bar, but its
Understanding page carries the only W3C prose defining the thing a heading
labels: a **section** is *"a self-contained portion of written content that
deals with one or more related topics or thoughts,"* and *"'heading' is used in
its general sense and includes titles and other ways to add a heading to
different types of content."* Note 2 excludes user-interface components.

**Techniques.** **PDF9**: *"Headings are marked up using the heading elements
(H, H1, H2, … H6) in the structure tree,"* sufficient for 1.3.1; its second
example states *"a Heading 2 should come after a Heading 1."* **G141**:
headings *"properly nested (e.g., h1 followed by h2, h2 followed by h2 or
h3)"*; test: *"Check that a heading for each section exists."* **G130**:
*"each heading identifies its section of the content."* **PDF6**: table
headers are `TH`. **PDF4**: artifacts are *"page header or footer information,
lines or other graphics separating sections of the page, or decorative
images."* The WAI tutorial adds: *"Skipping heading ranks can be confusing and
should be avoided where possible"*, and closing a subsection (`h4` then `h2`)
is not a skip.

## 2. The vocabulary — ISO 32000-1:2008 (PDF 1.7), clause 14.8

Quoted from the freely published text (Adobe, `PDF32000_2008.pdf`).

| Type | Definition (Table 333/335/337; 14.8.2.2) |
|---|---|
| **H** | *"A label for a subdivision of a document's content. It should be the first child of the division that it heads."* |
| **H1–H6** | *"Headings with specific levels, for use in conforming writers that cannot hierarchically nest their sections and thus cannot determine the level of a heading from its level of nesting."* |
| **P** | *"A low-level division of text."* |
| **Caption** | *"A brief portion of text describing a table or figure."* |
| **TH** | *"A table cell containing header text describing one or more rows or columns of the table."* |
| **TOC / TOCI** | *"A list made up of table of contents item entries"* / *"An individual member of a table of contents."* |
| **Sect** | *"A container for grouping related content elements"* — e.g. *"a heading, several introductory paragraphs, and two or more other sections nested within it."* |
| **Artifact** | *"graphics objects that are not part of the author's original content but rather are generated by the conforming writer in the course of pagination, layout, or other strictly mechanical processes."* |

**Strong vs weak structure (14.8.4.3.5).** *Strongly structured:* grouping
elements nest and *"the children of the grouping element should consist of a
heading (H), one or more paragraphs (P) … and perhaps one or more additional
grouping elements for nested subsections."* *Weakly structured:* *"the
organization of the material is not reflected in the logical structure;
however, it may be expressed by the use of headings with specific levels
(H1–H6)."* This product emits weak structure — `H1–H6`, never bare `H` — which
is what `Finish --renumber-headings` and the ladder ask already assume.

## 3. The conformance rules — PDF/UA-1 as veraPDF encodes them

ISO 14289-1 itself is paywalled; the rules below are quoted from the veraPDF
`PDFUA-1.xml` validation profile (GitHub, `veraPDF-validation-profiles`),
which is the second instrument this product already ships.

- **7.4.2, test 1** — *"For documents that are not strongly structured …
  heading tags shall be used as follows: If any heading tags are used, H1
  shall be the first"*; error *"Heading level %1 is skipped in a descending
  sequence of header levels."* → the level a reviewer assigns is **one deeper
  than the nearest open heading, or any shallower open level**, never a skip.
- **7.4.4, tests 1–3** — at most one `H` per node; a document is strongly *or*
  weakly structured, never both. → we never emit `H`.
- Table structure — *"TH element should be contained in TR element"*;
  *"Table element may contain a Caption element as its first or last kid."*
- *"TOCI element should be contained in TOC element."*
- Artifacts — tagged content and Artifact content do not nest inside each
  other.

**Not verified:** the Matterhorn Protocol (PDF Association) returned HTTP 403
on every URL tried. Its checkpoint 14 is what the 7.4 rules above implement;
it is not cited here because it could not be read.

## 4. The definition

A text block is labelled **heading** when all four hold:

1. **It labels a section** (2.4.10's sense; ISO `H`: *"a label for a
   subdivision of a document's content"*): the content after it, up to the next
   block of equal or shallower rank, is about the topic it names. If nothing
   follows it — no content before the next heading, or the end of the document
   — it labels nothing.
2. **It is the document's own content**, not the writer's pagination
   (ISO 14.8.2.2): a line that repeats on every page in the same place, a page
   number, a print date, a distribution stamp, a watermark is **Artifact**.
3. **It has no more specific standard type.** In this order: a table cell that
   describes a row or column is **TH**; text describing a figure, chart or table
   (whether above or below it) is **Caption**; an entry pointing into the
   document is **TOCI**; a list marker or bare numeral is **Lbl**; a quotation
   is **BlockQuote**. These win over `H` because the standard names them, and
   because PDF/UA nests `Caption` under `Table`/`Figure` and `TH` under `TR` —
   a heading there is a structure error, not a style choice.
4. **The visual cue is not the reason.** Prominence (size, weight, centring,
   isolation, capitals) is evidence a *writer* left, never the test (F43): an
   address, a date line, a byline, a name, a "Review period" field label, a
   value like "Q1" are `P` however they are set.

A block that fails any of the four is labelled with the type that fits:
`P`, `Artifact`, `TH`, `Caption`, `TOCI`, `Lbl`, `BlockQuote`. **The label is
the type, and "heading" is derived from it.** Recording the type rather than a
bare yes/no costs the reviewer one extra click and gives the model the
distinction the standard actually makes; every false-positive class becomes a
named confusion instead of one bucket.

**Level** is assigned only after eligibility, from the **approved** headings
before it in reading order: one deeper than the nearest open heading, or any
shallower open level. The document's first heading is `H1`. Because the
reviewer approves top-down, the prior stack a level is judged against is made of
*approved* decisions — the trustworthy context that Part 28 showed source tags
can never be (3/20 source levels matched truth).

Every case the spikes got wrong or argued about, with the type this definition
gives it and the clause that decides it.

| Text (document) | Type | Decided by |
|---|---|---|
| `Public Comment` (newcastle) | **H** | rule 1 — introduces the comment section |
| `CITY HALL, 1300 PERDIDO STREET` (nola) | **P** | rule 4; F43 ex. 1 (an address) |
| `Fee` (orono fee table) | **TH** | rule 3; ISO TH |
| `Receipts by hour`, `Workshop floor` (chart titles) | **Caption** | rule 3; ISO Caption *"describing a table or figure"* |
| `Figure 2: …` | **Caption** | rule 3 (Headings.java R4 already says so) |

| `Reception issues…` paragraph tagged H5 | **P** | rule 1 — it *is* the content, not a label for it |
| `Northwind Logistics · Internal · Page 1 of 1` | **Artifact** | rule 2; PDF4 |
| `Board discussion · not for circulation` (kicker) | **Artifact** | rule 2 — a distribution stamp |
| `3` (slide numeral) | **Lbl** (was `P`) | rule 3; Headings.java R2 |
| `Review period` (field label before a value) | **P** | rule 1 — labels a value, not a section; not a form field |
| `Q1` | **P** | rule 4 |
| Word `TOC Heading` with own outline level 9 (r23, r30) | **P** inside `TOC`/`Caption` | ISO TOC; AGENTS.md level-9 entry |
| 49 outline-levelled body sentences (r34-class) | **P** | rule 1 — the author styled paragraphs, not sections; F43 ex. 2 |
| Document title line | **H1** | 2.4.10 note 1 (*"includes titles"*); PDF9; PDF 1.7 has no `Title` type |
| A heading that is a full sentence (2.4.6 case) | **H** | eligibility holds; quality is 2.4.6's item |
| Empty heading (page break in a Heading2 paragraph) | not a block | nothing to label; `removeEmptyHeadings` |

Two rulings above change what the spike's cards said and are flagged so the
cards are re-labelled rather than silently disagreed with: chart titles were
`P` and are now `Caption`; the slide numeral was `P` and is now `Lbl`. Both are
still *not headings*, so no binary label moves.

## 6. What this changes about the model

1. **Predict the type, not a bit.** The role prompt's vocabulary
   (`H1–H6, P, LI, Table, Figure, Artifact`) lacks `Caption`, `TH`, `TOCI`,
   `Lbl` — the four types most of its false positives *are*. Add them; derive
   `heading` from the prediction. Every error is then a named confusion the
   standard recognises, and the FPR breaks down by which rule the model missed.
2. **Ask eligibility and level separately, in that order**, and give the level
   question the approved prior stack. Part 28 falsified source tags as context;
   approved decisions are a different input and have never been tried.
3. **Rule 2 needs cross-page evidence** (repeats in the same place). A single
   marked page cannot show it; the candidate card should carry a
   "repeats on N pages" fact computed deterministically, the way `Headings.java`
   R3 already detects page markers.
4. **Rule 1 needs what follows**, which the card's `Next:` field carries;
   rule 3 needs table/figure containment, which `ancestors`/`in_table_box`
   carry for tagged input and the layout tagger must supply for untagged.
5. **Deterministic rules stay in front of the model** where the standard is
   mechanical: R2 (no letters → `Lbl`/ornament), R3 (page markers →
   `Artifact`), R5 (inside a table box → not `H`), R4 (caption prefix →
   `Caption`). The model decides only what the rules cannot, and the evaluator
   reports the two populations separately.
6. **Word source.** The same definition applies to paragraphs; an approved
   heading becomes an outline level (`w:outlineLvl`) in the source and is
   re-converted — G141 satisfied at the source, no PDF surgery, and the
   converter's existing 148/148 heading fidelity carries it.

## 7. Reviewer procedure (the label form)

Shown a marked block with its page, previous and next blocks, and "repeats on
N pages":

1. Repeats in place, or page furniture? → **Artifact**.
2. Inside a table and describes a row/column? → **TH**. Describes a figure,
   chart or table? → **Caption**. Points to a page in this document? →
   **TOCI**. Bare number/bullet? → **Lbl**.
3. Does content after it belong to the topic it names? No → **P**.
4. Otherwise → **H**, then choose the level from the stack shown (one deeper
   than the open heading, or any shallower open level).

A reviewer who cannot decide marks **unsure**; those rows are excluded from the
test set *and counted* in the record. Inter-rater agreement is measured on a
200-card sample by a second reviewer before the test set is sealed; if
agreement is under 99 %, the disagreeing cases are adjudicated by adding a
ruling to §5, and the sample is re-labelled — never by dropping the harder
class.
