## What the finishing pass did

97 lines of Java, four document-catalog writes, no structure-tree work:
`/MarkInfo << /Marked true >>`, `/ViewerPreferences << /DisplayDocTitle true >>`,
`/Lang` from a supplied parameter, and an XMP stream carrying `pdfuaid:part=1`
plus `dc:title` copied from DocInfo where one exists.

That closed 7,502 of the 7,505 failures OpenDataLoader left. The three
survivors are all `7.1-9` (Metadata stream shall contain `dc:title`) on the
three documents authored without a title — the category-B case declined in
advance, behaving exactly as the classification predicted.

## The finding that matters most

**veraPDF passes documents whose every figure is described as "image 1".**

OpenDataLoader writes sequential placeholders — decoded from the tagged output
as `image 1`, `image 2`, `image 3`, `image 4` — as the `Alt` value for every
figure. It does this in `05-images-captioned`, where a human-written caption
sits directly beneath each image:

> Figure 1: The western quay at low tide, viewed from the depot apron. The berth
> allocated to Northwind is the second from the left.

**That document passes ua1 with zero failures.**

This is a WCAG 1.1.1 failure carrying a conformance certificate, produced by our
own pipeline on our own corpus. It is the clearest possible demonstration that
machine-checkable conformance is necessary and nowhere near sufficient — and it
independently confirms that the caption-extraction tier is not an optimisation
but a requirement, because the engine does not extract captions even when they
are directly adjacent.

## What worked

- **Reading order on the adversarial two-column layout.** Document 02 places its
  columns by absolute positioning with a heading band interrupting them and a
  callout straddling the gutter. Text extraction from the remediated file comes
  out in the correct logical order. XY-Cut++ earned its reputation here.
- **Structural tagging generally.** 7.1-3 alone was 7,665 failures across the
  corpus and is entirely gone.
- **Speed.** ~1.1 s per document end to end, including two JVM spawns and two
  full veraPDF runs. The JVM cost the research review flagged as unmeasured is
  121–321 ms per convert call — real, but not a problem at this scale.

## What did not work

- **OCR never fired.** Document 09 has the same zero bytes of extractable text
  after remediation as before. Every OCR option in the free CLI requires
  `--hybrid`, so the advertised 80+ language OCR lives in the separate Python
  hybrid server, not the Java path.
- **Table headers.** No `/TH` was produced anywhere, on either the ruled or the
  borderless table.
- **Borderless table detection.** Complete miss on defaults. `tableMethod:
  cluster` exists and was deliberately not used — tuning options until the
  numbers improve is the failure mode this spike was built to avoid — so
  whether it helps is an open question, not a negative result.

## Reading these numbers honestly

**The corpus biases optimistic.** Twelve documents rendered from clean semantic
HTML through Chromium are easier than InDesign, Word, or scanner output. Six are
authored adversarially, which pushes back on the bias but does not remove it.
**Treat every rate here as an upper bound.**

**Failure count is not a proxy for remediability.** Document 09 finished the
baseline with 5 failures, the fewest in the corpus, because it contains no text
to fail language checks on. A pipeline scored on failure count would rank the
one document it cannot remediate at all as its easiest case.

**Heading level accuracy was not rigorously verified.** Heading tags are present
in the output, but confirming levels against ground truth needs a proper
structure-tree walk, and the token-counting used here is not reliable enough to
support a claim. Open question, not a finding.
