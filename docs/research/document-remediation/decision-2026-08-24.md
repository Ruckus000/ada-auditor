# PROCEED WITH CONDITIONS

**As a service with tooling leverage. Not as automated software, and not yet as
anything sellable.**

Three conditions below are blocking. They are small, specific, and none is a
research project.

## What changed since yesterday's STOP

Three things, all evidence:

1. **We measured the wrong standard.** The gate was veraPDF PDF/UA conformance.
   The legal requirement is WCAG 2.1 AA. Six of the twenty remaining failures on
   real documents have no WCAG counterpart at all — and those six were the
   backbone of the STOP argument.
2. **The liability is 2%.** Of 195 table headers asserted into real documents,
   four are wrong, from one identifiable and fixable cause.
   [Verification](table-header-verification.md).
3. **The effort is roughly $5/page in labour.** ~6 min/page on a table-heavy
   document at a $50/hr loaded remediator. [Measurement](human-effort.md).

## And one thing that cuts the other way

Doing the work by hand corrected the headline. **98.1% of veraPDF failures
removed is not 98% of the work done.** On both documents finished by hand the
pipeline delivered tagged content, a reading order and a language — and **zero
headings and zero table header relationships**, which is most of what WCAG 1.3.1
actually asks for.

That is not a bug. `Headings.java` is demotion-only by deliberate safety design,
so it cannot supply a heading that was never tagged. Our safest decision is also
the ceiling on how much of 1.3.1 we can ever deliver.

## The numbers

| | |
|---|---|
| pipeline cost | ~$0 external, seconds per document, runs locally |
| human effort, measured | ~3–4 min/page simple · **~6 min/page table-heavy** |
| **and that is a floor** | neither test document had a figure, so alt text is unmeasured; contrast flagging has no tool; no form fields |
| labour cost at 6 min/page | ~$5/page |
| market price | $5–25/page · published vendor rates $4–11.50 |
| margin at $8/page | ~37%, before the unmeasured work above |
| competitive position | **parity with Adobe auto-tagging, not ahead of it** |

**Thin at the low end of the market, and the true number is higher than 6.**
That is the honest read, and it is why this is conditional rather than a go.

## Blocking conditions

**1. We cannot see contrast, and would ship a violation.** A real fee schedule
sets values in red on white at roughly 4.0:1 against a 4.5:1 minimum, apparently
to mark changes — which raises use-of-colour too. veraPDF's `ua1` profile does
not check it, our comparator does not check it, and every number in this project
is silent about it. **No document goes to a client until contrast is at least
detected and flagged.**

**2. Heading promotion, or an explicit disclaimer.** Zero headings on documents
that have three and twelve. Either the pipeline gains a way to promote headings
safely — which is an assertion, and everything this project learned says assert
carefully — or the service states plainly that heading structure is human work
and prices it in. Silently shipping documents with no headings while reporting
98% is not an option.

**3. Fix the table header inversion.** Known cause, four cells, one rule: a row
of pure currency values is not a header row. Small.

## What the product is

Not "send a PDF, get a remediated PDF." That is 0/9 and always was.

**A remediation service where our tooling removes the mechanical work, a person
does the judgement, and the client receives the fixed file plus a record of what
was checked, what was fixed, what remains, and who decided.** There is no safe
harbor; a documented good-faith process is what answers a demand letter, so the
evidence trail is the product and the file is the by-product.

The market timing is real and it is the strongest argument here: public entities
of 50,000+ must comply by **26 April 2027**, smaller ones by **26 April 2028**.
It is August 2026. The Title II exceptions mean the job is finite — identify the
actively-used subset, fix that, run a request process — which makes it scopeable
and sellable rather than a bottomless backlog.

## What I would not do

- **Not build the model tier for alt text.** The cost driver is not alt text, it
  is *deciding structure* — 10–15 of the 25 minutes on the table-heavy document.
  If a model tier is justified, that is what it should target, and that is a
  different design.
- **Not chase full PDF/UA conformance.** Six of twenty remaining failures are
  legally inert. Worth a line in the service description, not an engineering
  programme. *(Counter-argument recorded in `legal-standard.md`: they may still
  fail a client's own QA. Price it, don't build for it.)*
- **Not spend holdout 2.** Synthetic, and we now know synthetic flatters.

## What only you can decide

Whether there is appetite for a **services** business — one where margin depends
on a person's time and scaling means hiring — rather than the software product
originally specified. The evidence says the first can work and the second cannot.
That is a business-model question, not a technical one, and every number above
is an input to it rather than an answer.

## The next concrete step, if it is a go

One pilot: a single small municipality's actively-used document set, priced per
page, delivered with the evidence record. That is the only remaining way to find
out whether ~6 min/page holds when the documents are not ones we chose.
