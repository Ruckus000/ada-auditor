## Why this round
Round 2 measured the training gap as depth, not just volume. The SFT had 255 H rows from 40 documents, but only 25 were H3 and 1 was H4. All 39 H3/H4 headings in validation are in a single document, r12, and 11 of its 15 H4 headings were missed (H→P citing rule 4). On this validation set a heading-depth data gap and an r12-specific gap could not be separated, because every deep heading is in r12 and r12 is almost every heading — more data was the fix that would separate them.

Real harvest yield was too low to supply that depth quickly: cohort 4 kept 37 of 531 downloaded PDFs (37 hosts targeted, 15 hosts realized), and municipal DocumentCenter PDFs were judged the wrong population for a heading dataset. The next data is therefore a planted Word cohort — generated, not harvested — built specifically for H3/H4 depth. By ruling P3, this is training data and a sanity population only, never a proof population: every pass/fail statement in this record is made on the real populations (build 4, cohort 3, cohort 4).

## The planted cohort
- 100 docx across 8 template families, generated by `experiments/qwen-role-decisions/labels/planted_cohort.mjs` at seed base 20260914 (ruling P1); byte-reproducible from the script and seed list (101 files verified identical on regeneration).
- Declared heading counts (as authored): H1 100, H2 439, H3 874, H4 571.
- Keys (`out/keys-c5`, `--label-source planted`, ruling P2's `planted-<family>.invalid` hosting): 1,490 cards, match rate 0.938, labelled H3 338 and H4 173. The gap between the declared counts and the labelled counts is candidate recall in the ODL-retagged copy, not a keys or matching defect — the key tree itself shows 100/479/934/571 against the declared 100/439/874/571.
- Heading texts: 39.8 % of H3 strings and 48.3 % of H4 strings are distinct (the rest repeat across the template families).
- Heading share of planted rows is 50.5 % of 1,490. Combined with the real train split this brings train H-share to 32.3 %, against roughly 22 % on real documents alone — registered as a risk to real-population false positives (ruling P4), which prediction P-c below is built to catch.

## Registered prediction
Judged pass/fail as written below, not against the Stage 1 bar's 99 %/1 % thresholds. Both `adapter-r2` and `adapter-r3` are evaluated on the same rows in every comparison.

- **P-a.** On the combined real validation (279 rows: build 4 + cohort 3 + cohort 4), r3's accuracy is greater than r2's accuracy on the same 279 rows.
- **P-b.** On r12 alone: r3 misses fewer than 11 of the 15 true H4 headings (r2 missed 11), and r3's H3 recall on r12 exceeds r2's H3 recall on r12.
- **P-c.** On the combined real validation, r3's false-positive rate is ≤ r2's false-positive rate. Also reported per population (build 4, cohort 3, cohort 4) individually; any population where r3's FP rate is higher than r2's is named explicitly.
- **P-d.** Heading-level exactness by depth (H1, H2, H3, H4 reported separately) for both adapters. For H3 and H4 specifically, r3's level exactness is at least r2's.
- **Not predicted:** whether the Stage 1 bar (accuracy ≥ 0.99, FP rate ≤ 1 %, level exactness ≥ 95 %) is met. It is expected not to be met.
